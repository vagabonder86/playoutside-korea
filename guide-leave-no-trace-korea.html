Leave No Trace on Korean Trails

The seven Leave No Trace principles, and what they mean on Korea's busy mountains, village paths and campsites.

By PlayOutside Korea · Published Nov 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Korea's mountains are busy, and the trails pass through villages, farms and national parks. Leave No Trace is how we keep them good to visit. Here are the seven principles, and what they look like on Korean trails.

1. Plan ahead and prepare

Check the route, the weather and any trail closures before you go. In Korea's national parks some trails close at certain times of year, so check the park's official notices first. Plan where you will sleep before you set out.

2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces

Stay on the marked trail, even when it is crowded, and do not cut corners on switchbacks. Camp only at designated campsites or where camping is clearly allowed. On village sections, such as the Jirisan Dulle-gil, the path often runs beside fields and homes: stay on it.

3. Dispose of waste properly

Carry a rubbish bag and take everything home, including food scraps and fruit peel. Use toilets at trailheads and campsites wherever you can.

4. Leave what you find

Leave plants, stones and anything else you find where it is, so the next person can enjoy it too. Take photos instead.

5. Minimise campfire impacts

Do not make open fires on the trail. Cook on a stove, only where it is safe and allowed, and never leave it unattended. Wildfire is a real risk in the dry seasons.

6. Respect wildlife

Watch animals from a distance and never feed them. Store food and rubbish where animals cannot get to it, especially overnight.

7. Be considerate of other visitors

Korean trails can be busy at weekends. Keep to one side so others can pass, keep music off, and greet people on the way. On village paths, keep your voice down and respect private land.

The simple version

Stay on the trail, camp where it is allowed, cook on a stove, and carry out everything you carry in. Leave every place the way you found it, or better.
